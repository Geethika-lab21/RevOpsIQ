1. Executive summary and product definition
Product name: RevOpsIQ (working name). Category: B2B SaaS revenue operations, strategy and business operations
intelligence platform.
RevOpsIQ is a web application for a fictional or configurable B2B SaaS company. It centralises market and competitor intelligence,
target-account research, opportunity pipeline tracking, customer onboarding and product adoption, revenue and unit-economics
metrics, business reviews, and operational action tracking.
Continuous operating loop: Understand the market → prioritise accounts → manage opportunities → onboard customers →
monitor adoption and revenue → review performance → assign actions → measure follow-through.
Modules must share data. A market segment links to accounts; accounts link to opportunities; Closed Won opportunities convert to
customers; customers have onboarding and adoption records; customer and deal data feed business metrics; insights create
recommendations and assigned actions.
This is a portfolio-grade prototype, not a production CRM replacement. It must implement real CRUD operations, persistence,
calculations, filtering, workflows, validation and export. Static mock data is permitted only as clearly labelled seeded demo data.
Primary product outcome
Help a business operations team decide where to focus, understand what is happening and why, and turn insights into assigned,
trackable actions.
2. Problem, goals and non-goals
B2B SaaS teams often manage market research, account lists, sales opportunities, onboarding milestones, product usage,
financial performance and weekly action items in separate tools. Fragmentation makes it difficult to maintain a consistent business
view, identify bottlenecks, compare segments, surface customer risk and ensure decisions lead to execution.
Goals
• Centralise core business records in a relational data model.
• Support market and competitor research with source notes, assumptions and segment scoring.
• Support account qualification and opportunity tracking with transparent scoring and stage history.

4. Six functional modules
Module 1 — Market Intelligence
Purpose: Understand which industries, segments and competitor positions may represent attractive opportunities. Screens: Market
Overview, Segments, Competitors, Research Sources, Opportunity Scoring and Segment Detail.
• Create, edit, archive, search and filter market segments. Fields: name, industry, geography, company-size band, use case,
estimated target-organisation count, urgency/problem indicators, buying complexity, notes and status.
• Store TAM, SAM and SOM with currency, period, method, assumptions, source, confidence and review date. Estimates must never
appear as verified facts without methodology and source.
• Create competitor profiles: company, website, target segments, product category, stated capabilities, pricing notes, differentiators,
strengths, weaknesses, source URL, observation date and confidence.
• Compare competitors in a matrix. Distinguish sourced facts from analyst observations.
• Score segments with configurable criteria: market attractiveness, urgency, product fit, accessibility and competitive intensity. Show
score breakdown and rationale.
• Link target accounts to one or more segments.
Market sizing: Support top-down or bottom-up methods selected by the user. Example bottom-up calculation: number of target
organisations × estimated annual contract value. Display assumptions and method next to the result.
Acceptance: A user can create a segment, attach evidence, compare competitors, calculate a transparent score and link target
accounts.

Module 2 — Account Intelligence & Sales Pipeline
Purpose: Maintain target account records and track opportunities from research through close. Screens: Account Directory,
Account Detail, Account Scorecard, Opportunity Pipeline, Deal Detail, Pipeline Forecast and Stalled Deals.
• Account fields: organisation, domain, industry, geography, employee/revenue band (optional), segment, use case, pain point, fit
notes, source, owner, status, priority and last-researched date.
• Contact fields: name, role, department, optional business email, account, source and notes. Do not require personal contact data for
seeded demo records.
• Configurable account-fit score using segment fit, use-case fit, size fit, geography fit, urgency and data completeness. Show
component breakdown; label as rule-based, not ML prediction.
• Opportunity fields: name, account, owner, stage, estimated deal value, currency, probability, expected close date, source, next step,
next-step due date, last activity, loss reason and notes.
• Default stages: Target → Qualified → Discovery → Evaluation / Demo → Proposal → Negotiation → Closed Won / Closed Lost.
Admin can configure stages and probabilities.
• Provide Kanban and sortable table views; retain stage history with timestamp and actor.
• Weighted pipeline = open deal value × probability. Label as estimate, not booked revenue.
• Flag stale opportunities, missing next steps, overdue next steps and past expected close dates.
• Filter by owner, segment, stage, geography, deal size and close period.
• Closed Won can create/link a customer without duplicating the account. Closed Lost records loss reason and optional competitor.
Acceptance: Create and score an account, create an opportunity, move it through stages, record next steps, view weighted
pipeline and identify stalled deals.
Module 3 — Customer Success, Onboarding & Adoption
Purpose: Track the transition from Closed Won to successful onboarding and ongoing adoption. Screens: Customer Directory,
Customer Detail, Onboarding Board, Adoption Overview and Health & Risk Queue.
• Customer fields: linked account, source opportunity, start date, plan/tier, contract value, renewal date, owner and lifecycle status.
• Configurable onboarding milestones: Kickoff Scheduled, Requirements / Configuration, Integration, Testing, Training, Go-Live and
Adoption Review.
• Each milestone has owner, due date, status, completion date, blocker and notes.
• Track adoption observations such as active users, feature usage, usage frequency, successful workflows or training completion.
Data may be manually entered or CSV-imported; do not imply live telemetry integration.
• Calculate transparent health score from configurable components: adoption, onboarding progress, support issues, engagement and
renewal proximity. Show component contributions and missing-data warnings.
• Flag falling usage, overdue onboarding and upcoming renewal for review.
• Track customer risks, mitigation plan, next contact date and owner. Create follow-up actions linked to customer.
Acceptance: Convert a won deal into a customer, create onboarding milestones, record adoption, inspect health/risk explanation
and assign follow-up actions.

5. End-to-end workflows
Workflow A — Market opportunity to target account
1. Create a segment and record definition, geography, use case and evidence.
2. Enter market-sizing assumptions and sources; calculate estimates with visible labels.
3. Add competitors and compare capabilities/positioning.
4. Score segment and inspect component breakdown.
5. Create/import accounts and link them to the segment.
6. Review account fit score and missing research fields.
7. Select suitable accounts for opportunity creation.
Workflow B — Account to closed deal
1. Create/import account and record research evidence.
2. Calculate transparent fit score.
3. Create opportunity with value, stage, probability, expected close and next step.
4. Update stage/activity; retain stage history.
5. Recalculate pipeline and weighted pipeline.
6. Flag stale or overdue opportunities.
7. Closed Won converts to customer; Closed Lost records loss reason.
Workflow C — Closed Won to customer adoption
1. Link won opportunity to customer record.
2. Create onboarding plan from template.
3. Assign milestone owners and due dates.
4. Update status and blockers.
5. Enter/import adoption observations.
6. Recalculate health score with visible components and completeness.
7. Create risk recommendations and follow-up actions.
Workflow D — Business review to action
1. Create weekly/monthly review.
2. Display KPI snapshots and changes for period.
3. Record findings, decisions and unresolved issues.
4. Create actions with owner, priority and due date.
5. Monitor progress, overdue items and blockers.
6. Record completion notes and date.
7. Finalise review and preserve snapshot.
Workflow E — Signal to recommendation
1. Detect a documented business condition.
2. Store signal and evidence.
3. User reviews rationale and linked records.
4. User accepts, dismisses or snoozes.
5. Accepted recommendation becomes assigned action.
6. Completion/dismissal outcome remains available for review.

Account fit score: Σ(criterion score × criterion weight) ÷ Σ(applicable weights), normalised to 0–100. Criteria may include segment,
use-case, size, geography, urgency and data completeness. Show the breakdown and mark incomplete where required inputs are
missing.
Customer health score: Configurable weighted combination of adoption, onboarding progress, support/risk signals, engagement
and renewal proximity. Missing data must not silently count as healthy; show completeness and either adjust applicable weights or
mark score incomplete.
Action priority score: Configurable impact × urgency × confidence model. Show inputs and label it as a prioritisation aid.
• Define time window and denominator for every percentage.
• Keep pipeline, bookings, recognised revenue and recurring revenue distinct.
• Return Not Available for empty denominators.
• Use canonical stage mapping for win rate and stage conversion.
• Do not infer recurring revenue from a one-time deal value.
• Use snapshots/event history for historical comparisons.
• Document defaults and allow admin configuration.
8. AI / recommendation layer and guardrails
Core calculations and rule triggers must be deterministic and testable; the app must work without an external LLM. Optional LLM
features may summarise already-calculated evidence.
Permitted AI-assisted functions
• Summarise a market segment from stored research records and cite source records.
• Draft business-review narrative from KPI snapshots, decisions and notes.
• Explain why an account, opportunity or customer was flagged using rule evidence.
• Suggest possible next steps for human review.
• Convert meeting notes into draft action items requiring confirmation before saving.
Guardrails
• Never invent market statistics, competitor capabilities, pricing, customer feedback or metrics.
• Distinguish sourced facts, imported data, calculated metrics, assumptions and AI suggestions.
• Link generated summaries to records used.
• Never let AI overwrite structured source data automatically.
• Require human approval before actions or record changes.
• Do not send secrets or unnecessary personal data to an LLM.
• If evidence is absent, say information is unavailable.
• Keep deterministic rules separate from generated narrative.

15. AI-agent operating instructions
Treat this synopsis as the single source of truth for the first implementation.
Before coding
1. Read the entire specification.
2. Inspect existing repository and report structure, stack, working features and broken areas. Do not overwrite working code blindly.
3. Create a checklist mapping every module and acceptance criterion to files, APIs, models and tests.
4. Propose database ERD and API resource list before implementation.
5. Identify ambiguities and state defaults explicitly; do not invent external facts.
6. Create phased plan and implement one complete vertical slice first.
During implementation
• Work in small phases; run tests and fix failures before proceeding.
• Do not create only frontend mockups. Functional screens must use real APIs and persistent data.
• Never hardcode dashboard metrics; calculate them from the database.
• No dead buttons. Implement the action or disable it with explanation.
